## Participant information and consent

We are a university project team studying how one department advertises internships, handles applications and follows student progress. Participation is voluntary and has no effect on selection, marks or access to opportunities. You may skip any question or stop the session. Please use general or invented examples and avoid names, grades, credentials, employer secrets and identifiable applications.

We would like to take brief written notes for requirements analysis. Reports will use participant codes and summarized findings. Recording is optional and needs separate permission; without explicit permission, do not record. Store consent/contact details separately from notes, restrict raw-note access to the project team, and agree a deletion date with the department before recruitment. Do not publish raw notes or participant identities in Jira or a public repository. Explain any limits on withdrawal before participation and agree the final anonymization date; do not promise removal of already unidentifiable aggregate data.

## Coordinator guide

1. Walk through a recent internship from notice to completion using an anonymized example. Which person does each step and which tools hold the record?
2. Who approves organizations, opportunities and mentors? What makes a proposal ready or causes it to be returned?
3. What fields, eligibility information and deadlines must students see? Which timezone governs the deadline?
4. How do you detect duplicate or late applications, and what explanations should students receive?
5. Does the organization recommend a decision or make the final decision? Who may change a visible status?
6. What does each status mean, and what should a student do next? Which internal notes must stay private?
7. Can students apply to multiple opportunities or hold multiple active placements? What happens after selection?
8. Who agrees learning goals and mentors? What evidence is needed to complete a placement?
9. How are weekly check-ins, missed submissions and mentor responses handled? Which calendar defines a week?
10. Which pending actions take most effort to find? If permitted, time an anonymized triage task; otherwise record timing as unavailable.
11. What student information is necessary, who may read it, and what storage/retention approvals are needed?
12. Which proposed feature would you remove first to meet 27 November? Who can validate the resulting scope?

Probe neutrally: What happened next? Can you show a blank form? Is that formal policy or usual practice? What exceptions occur? Do not assume a proposed pain point exists.

## Student guide

1. How do you discover approved opportunities and judge relevance, eligibility and deadline?
2. Walk through an anonymized application. How do you know it was submitted successfully?
3. How do you find the current decision, last update and next action? What is unclear, if anything?
4. What would Submitted, Under Review, Shortlisted, Selected and Declined mean to you?
5. Do you apply to several opportunities? What should happen if you already have an active placement?
6. How would you record work completed, a blocker and a next step against a learning goal?
7. What mentor feedback would be useful, and who should see it?
8. Which devices, connection limits, keyboard or assistive-technology needs should the prototype support?
9. If access is granted, time finding one application's next action; otherwise record no baseline timing.
10. Which information should never be requested or shown? Is there anything we misunderstood?

End by reading back the process and uncertainties. Ask whether the summary reflects the participant's account, without treating one opinion as department-wide policy.
